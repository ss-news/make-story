The Legend of the Marble Mountains

Long ago, on the coast of Quang Nam, near today's Da Nang, an old man lived alone in a small thatched hut beside the sea.

One stormy night, a giao long, a sea dragon, emerged from the crashing waves. It crawled onto the shore, laid an enormous egg in the sand, and disappeared beneath the dark water.

The next morning, Kim Quy, the Golden Turtle Spirit, appeared. He buried the egg beneath the sand and asked the old man to watch over it.

“Keep this egg safe,” the spirit said, giving him a magical claw. “If danger comes, this will protect you.”

“As long as I am here,” the old man promised, “no harm will come to it.”

One day, a soldier passing along the beach noticed the strange egg.

“What kind of egg grows this big?” he said. “Let’s break it open!”

“Leave it alone!” the old man cried. “It is under the protection of the spirits!”

The soldier ignored him and stepped closer.

The old man raised the magical claw. In an instant, he transformed into a fierce tiger. His roar thundered across the beach.

“A tiger!” the soldier screamed, fleeing without another glance.

As time passed, the egg continued to grow. It pushed up the sand around it, lifting the old man’s hut higher and higher.

“Golden Turtle Spirit!” the old man called. “Please help me!”

Then a great crack echoed through the air.

The shell split apart, forming rocky walls and deep caves. From inside the egg, a little girl stepped into the sunlight.

The old man stared in astonishment.

“Where did you come from, little one?”

The girl looked up at him.

“Grandfather, I’m hungry.”

He took her into his home and raised her as his own granddaughter. And the mountain helped him care for her.

Milk trickled from the rocks to feed the child. Monkeys brought her ripe fruit. Birds gathered silk to make her clothes.

She grew with extraordinary speed, soon becoming a young woman.

The old man taught her to build a home, plant rice, and shoot a bow.

“Pull the string back,” he told her. “Keep your eyes on the target. Now let go!”

The arrow flew straight and struck its mark.

“I did it, Grandfather!” she cried. “I hit it!”

For a time, they lived peacefully beside the sea.

But word of the beautiful young woman spread, and one night, pirates landed at the foot of the mountain.

“Seize the girl!” their leader shouted. “This mountain belongs to us now!”

“Grandfather!” she called. “Pirates!”

Before the intruders could reach their home, flames burst from the rocks. Fire swept across their path, surrounding them in a blazing wall.

“Fire!” the pirates cried. “Get back!”

Terrified, they fled to their boats.

The young woman’s kindness soon became as well known as her beauty. When a terrible fever swept through a nearby village, the villagers came to her for help.

“Please,” one woman begged. “Our families are growing weaker every day.”

The young woman scattered brightly colored stones across a garden. Wherever a stone landed, a flower with five petals sprang from the earth.

In the legend, these magical flowers healed the sick, and the people recovered.

News of her deeds eventually reached the royal court. The king sent a messenger to the mountain.

“His Majesty invites you to the capital,” the messenger announced. “He wishes you to marry the prince.”

The Golden Turtle Spirit returned with word that the Dragon King, her true father, had given his blessing.

Soon, a palanquin arrived to carry the young woman to her new home.

Before leaving, she turned to the old man who had raised her.

“Please take care of yourself, Grandfather.”

“Go, my child,” he said gently. “My work here is done.”

After she departed, the old man returned the magical claw to the Golden Turtle Spirit. Then he climbed onto the great turtle’s back, and together they vanished into the sea.

The stone mountain remained, its caves preserving the memory of the enormous egg and the child born within it.

And so, the story tells us, the Marble Mountains, Ngu Hanh Son, came to stand beside the sea.

Today, the Marble Mountains, known as Ngu Hanh Son, are in Da Nang, on the coast of central Vietnam.
